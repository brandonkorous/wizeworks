# WizeWorks LLC — Landing Page Strategy

**Status:** Recommended production landing-page structure  
**Primary objective:** Turn visitors into people who explore the WizeWorks portfolio, understand what WizeWorks is, trust the company behind the products, and start a conversation when they are ready.  
**Primary brand statement:** **We make things that work.**

---

# 1. What This Page Must Accomplish

WizeWorks is not a single SaaS product.

It is the maker and parent company behind a portfolio of distinct products:

- Piggles
- sparx
- AGCONN
- kanNINJA
- jotacular
- SilicaUI

Because of that, the homepage has a more complex job than a conventional SaaS landing page.

Within the first few moments, a visitor should understand:

1. **What WizeWorks is**
2. **What WizeWorks makes**
3. **Why the work is credible**
4. **What makes the company different**
5. **Where to go next**
6. **How to start a conversation**

The page must convert two different types of visitors:

### Product explorers
People who arrive because they want to understand the companies/products WizeWorks owns or creates.

Their likely action is:

> **Explore our products**

### Partnership / company-intent visitors
People evaluating WizeWorks itself, the team, the work, or a possible partnership.

Their likely action is:

> **Start a conversation**

The page should support both, but one action should always have stronger visual hierarchy in each context.

---

# 2. Conversion Principles

Current conversion-design research consistently favors **clarity over cleverness**.

A high-performing page should make its primary promise, audience, visual proof, and next action obvious quickly. It should avoid forcing visitors to decipher what the company does.

For WizeWorks, this means:

- Keep the hero statement extremely simple
- Use a clear supporting sentence
- Give the page a dominant primary CTA
- Let secondary actions remain visually quieter
- Show real products instead of describing capabilities abstractly
- Put evidence close to major claims
- Reduce friction as the visitor moves toward contact
- Use repeated CTA language consistently
- Design for mobile and performance from the beginning

This does **not** mean WizeWorks should look like a generic conversion template.

Conversion clarity and strong art direction are compatible.

---

# 3. Recommended Navigation

WizeWorks is a corporate/product-studio homepage rather than a campaign-specific ad landing page, so keeping useful navigation is appropriate.

Recommended primary navigation:

- Products
- Work
- About
- Insights

Primary header CTA:

> **Let's build**

Alternative:

> **Start a conversation**

The CTA should be visually distinct from the other navigation items.

Do not overload the navigation with:

- individual product links
- careers
- press
- documentation
- legal
- social media
- multiple equal-weight CTAs

Those belong in menus, secondary pages, or the footer.

# 4. Section 01 — Hero

## Purpose

Answer the visitor's biggest questions immediately:

- Who are you?
- What do you do?
- Why should I care?
- What can I do next?

## Recommended Content

Primary headline:

> **We make  
> things that  
> work.**

There is **no eyebrow**. An earlier draft opened with the label
"A product studio & parent company" stacked above the headline; eyebrows are
banned (see §29a). What it said was worth saying, so it became the first
sentence of the supporting copy instead — something a person reads rather than
a label they have to decode.

The final word **work.** should stay in charcoal. Warm taupe on the paper ground
measures 2.42:1, which fails WCAG AA for both normal and large text, and §23
requires compliant contrast. The two-tone identity is carried by the mark, which
sits beside the headline and makes the point without a contrast failure.

Supporting copy:

> WizeWorks builds thoughtful products, systems, and brands that solve real problems and create lasting impact.

Primary CTA:

> **Explore our products**

Secondary CTA:

> **View our work**

## Visual

Keep the hero direction already selected:

- oversized physical 3D WizeWorks maker's mark
- warm-paper / white environment
- dark charcoal and warm taupe materials
- extremely subtle drafting or topographic construction lines
- soft physical shadows
- enough empty space for the mark to breathe

The logo should feel like a **physical object built by WizeWorks**, not a floating SVG enlarged to fill space.

## Why This Works

The headline is short enough to absorb almost immediately.

The supporting sentence removes ambiguity.

The visual demonstrates the brand rather than adding unrelated decoration.

The primary CTA directs visitors to the most important proof WizeWorks has: **the products themselves**.

# 5. Section 02 — Immediate Proof Strip

## Purpose

The hero makes a promise.

The next block should immediately provide evidence that WizeWorks actually makes things.

Do **not** jump directly into generic company philosophy.

Recommended framing:

> **Made by WizeWorks**

Then display the current portfolio as recognizable brand marks:

- Piggles
- sparx
- AGCONN
- kanNINJA
- jotacular
- SilicaUI

Optional supporting line:

> Six distinct products. One shared standard: they work.

(The site derives this count from the product data, so it cannot drift again.)

## Behavior

Desktop:

- horizontal brand rail
- restrained color
- generous spacing

Mobile:

- two-column or horizontally scrollable rail

Each brand should be clickable.

## Important

The WizeWorks maker's mark remains neutral.

Each product keeps its own color.

This is the first visual demonstration of the brand-architecture principle:

> **WizeWorks provides the canvas. Products provide the expression.**

# 6. Section 03 — Product Portfolio

## Purpose

This is arguably the most important section after the hero.

WizeWorks should prove what it means by "we make things that work."

Do not present the products as five identical SaaS cards.

Each product should feel like its own mini world.

## Section Header

Suggested:

> **Things we've made.**

Alternative:

> **Our products**

Supporting line:

> Different problems. Different people. Built with the same level of care.

## Recommended Product Card Content

Each card should contain:

1. Product logo
2. One-sentence positioning
3. Category
4. Real product interface or visual
5. One short outcome/use-case statement
6. Explore link

Example pattern:

### Piggles
**Business software for people who have a business to run.**

Product UI visual.

> Run the everyday parts of a business without feeling like you're operating enterprise software.

CTA:

> **Explore Piggles →**

## Visual Strategy

Rather than equal tiny cards, use a richer composition.

Recommended:

### Desktop
- asymmetric grid
- one or two larger feature cards
- smaller supporting cards
- intentional asymmetry
- product screenshots embedded into each brand world

### Mobile
- stacked full-width product stories

The section should feel more like a **design portfolio** than a pricing comparison table.

# 7. Section 04 — What WizeWorks Believes

## Purpose

After visitors see the portfolio, explain the common thread.

This is where WizeWorks becomes more than a holding-company logo.

Suggested headline:

> **Different products. Same obsession.**

Or:

> **Useful beats impressive.**

Recommended principles:

### Real problems first
We start with something worth solving.

### Designed around people
Technology should adapt to the people using it.

### Built to last
We build systems that can grow rather than disposable experiments.

### Details matter
Good products usually feel obvious because somebody cared about everything underneath them.

## Presentation

Avoid four generic icon cards.

Better:

- oversized typography
- alternating text and visual fragments
- close-ups of products
- material/physical images
- annotated details
- short statements

This section should communicate personality.

---

# 8. Section 05 — How We Make Things

## Purpose

Show the process without pretending product creation is a magical agency framework.

Recommended framing:

> **How we work**

Suggested stages:

### 01 — Strategy
Start with the people, problem, constraints, and opportunity.

### 02 — Design
Turn the problem into a clear product direction.

### 03 — Systems
Design and engineer the thing that has to keep working after everyone stops paying attention.

### 04 — Launch
Put it in front of real people, then keep making it better.

## Why Four Steps

Four stages is the canonical WizeWorks sequence.

It stays practical instead of inflating ordinary product work into an agency framework, and each stage is something a visitor can actually picture happening.

Production software is not complete at launch — but that belongs inside **Launch** as ongoing work, not as a separate terminal stage. The process is not a funnel that ends.

## Visual

Do not use four generic feature boxes.

Consider:

- a continuous horizontal build line
- drafting marks
- changing physical materials
- product artifacts
- sketches → wireframes → UI → launched product → telemetry/feedback

This should feel like watching something get made.

---

# 9. Section 06 — Selected Work / Stories

## Purpose

This is where the page gains real credibility.

Product logos alone establish existence.

Stories establish **results**.

Current landing-page research strongly supports using specific, relevant proof near conversion points rather than generic logo walls alone.

## Recommended Format

Feature 2–3 stories.

Each should contain:

- product/company
- real problem
- what WizeWorks built
- result
- image
- measurable metric when truthful and available

Example structure:

> **From idea to operating product**
>
> How [Product] turned [specific problem] into [specific outcome].

Metrics can include:

- number of businesses using it
- time saved
- revenue generated
- users served
- jobs connected
- transactions processed
- uptime
- deployment velocity

## Critical Rule

Never invent numbers.

If strong metrics do not exist yet, use qualitative proof rather than fake quantitative proof.

# 10. Section 07 — Social Proof

## Purpose

Reduce uncertainty.

If WizeWorks has strong testimonials, this section should appear before the final CTA.

Possible proof sources:

- customers
- founders
- businesses using the products
- partners
- product users
- industry relationships

## Strong Testimonial Format

Use:

- real name
- real role
- real company/product
- photo when appropriate
- specific statement

Avoid:

> "WizeWorks is amazing!"

Prefer something like:

> "[Specific product] replaced three separate workflows for our team and let us run the business without adding more administrative overhead."

## Volume

If only one great testimonial exists, feature it prominently.

If several strong testimonials exist, show 3–6.

Do not fill space with weak proof.

# 11. Section 08 — The WizeWorks Story

## Purpose

Visitors evaluating the company itself will eventually ask:

Suggested headline:

> **We build because we keep finding things that should work better.**

Content should briefly explain:

- why WizeWorks exists
- why it builds across very different industries
- what connects the products
- what the maker's mark means

This is also the right location to explain:

Not as logo-design trivia, but as philosophy:

> A shared foundation can create very different things.

CTA:

> **About WizeWorks →**

- launch stories
- engineering lessons
- business-building observations

# 14. Section 11 — Final CTA

- what WizeWorks is
- what it makes

- how it works
- evidence that the products exist
- proof/results
- who is behind it

## Recommended Dark CTA Band

Use a strong charcoal section.

Headline:

> **Let's build something worth using.**

Alternative:

> **Have an idea? Let's build it.**

Supporting copy:

> Have a problem worth solving, a product worth building, or a business that needs something better? We'd like to hear about it.

Primary CTA:

> **Start a conversation**

Optional expectation-setting microcopy:

> No pitch deck required.

or

> Tell us what you're trying to solve.

This makes the interaction feel lower pressure.

# 16. CTA Strategy

The homepage should not contain five competing high-contrast CTA styles.

Recommended action hierarchy:

## Primary
> **Explore our products**

Use near the hero.

## Company / Partnership
> **Start a conversation**

Use in the navigation and final CTA.

## Secondary
> **View our work**

Use as text/outline treatment.

## Tertiary
> Explore [Product]

Used within product stories.

The visual hierarchy should make it obvious which action belongs to each stage of the page.

- real active products
- customer quotes
- measurable results
- founder/company identity
- clear contact information
- legal company name

- product links
- public documentation

# 18. Conversion Friction

Do not ask for too much information too early.

If the final CTA opens a contact form, recommended initial fields:

- Name
- Email
- What are you working on?

Optional:

- Company

Avoid initially requiring:

- budget
- phone number
- company size
- timeframe
- detailed project category
- long questionnaires

More qualification can happen after initial interest exists.

# 20. Interaction & Motion

Production motion should tell the story of building.

Potential interactions:

### Hero
Maker's-mark pieces settle into place on initial load.

### Portfolio
Product color subtly enters as each product comes into focus.

### Product cards
Screenshots move or animate slightly on hover.

### Process
A construction line progresses as the user scrolls.

### Maker's mark
Negative space becomes emphasized as sections transition.

### Final CTA
Subtle physical depth/lighting shift rather than a generic button animation.

All motion must respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

---

# 21. Mobile Strategy

Do not treat mobile as a compressed desktop page.

Important adjustments:

- Hero becomes stacked
- Maker's mark remains visually important
- Product cards become individual full-width stories
- Shorten supporting copy
- Keep CTA visible without making it sticky everywhere
- Avoid horizontal process diagrams that become unreadable
- Load media lazily
- Preserve strong typography while preventing awkward line breaks

The first mobile screen should still communicate:

> WizeWorks  
> We make things that work.  
> What WizeWorks makes  
> What to do next

---

# 22. Performance

A high-converting design that loads slowly is not production-grade.

Targets:

- optimized responsive images
- modern image formats
- lazy-load below-fold media
- minimal client-side JavaScript
- avoid autoplay video unless truly necessary
- preload only critical fonts/assets
- prevent layout shift
- test Core Web Vitals
- keep interaction latency low

The 3D hero logo should be delivered efficiently.

If a lightweight rendered asset can produce the intended visual result, do not ship an unnecessary heavy WebGL scene.

---

# 23. Accessibility

Minimum requirements:

- semantic headings
- keyboard navigation
- visible focus states
- sufficient color contrast
- descriptive link/CTA text
- alt text for meaningful images
- decorative images hidden from assistive technology
- reduced-motion support
- no color-only communication
- accessible forms and validation

The visual sophistication must never reduce usability.

# 24a. Banned Design Elements

These are bans, not preferences. They outrank any layout that would look tidier
with them.

## Eyebrows

No small label stacked above a heading — no uppercase tracked kicker, no
category tag, no "section 01" chip standing in for a heading.

An eyebrow is a heading that lost its nerve. If the text matters, it belongs in
the heading or in a sentence a person actually reads. If it does not matter, it
should not be on the page. In practice eyebrows also arrive at 10–12px and
uppercase, which is the least readable combination available, so the ban and the
16px floor are the same argument twice.

Section headings must stand on their own. "Four stages, and none of them end at
launch." needs no "HOW WE WORK" above it.

## Custom font sizes

Only the named steps in `design.md` §11. No hand-written `clamp()`, no arbitrary
pixel values, no inline or computed `font-size`. Nothing below 14px, and 16px is
the body default.

If a layout only works with smaller text, the layout is wrong — not the floor.
Shrinking type to fit is never the fix, because density is spacing, not type
size (`design.md` §12a). Add room instead, or cut something.

## Structure that decorates rather than informs

Numbering, rules, and labels have to encode something true. Four parallel
principles are not a sequence, so numbering them 01–04 is decoration. Four
process stages **are** a sequence, so numbering those is information.

# 25. What Should NOT Be on the Homepage

Avoid:

- giant feature matrices
- fake stats
- fake testimonials
- generic "trusted by" logos with no relationship
- pricing unless WizeWorks itself sells a standardized service
- detailed product feature lists
- full corporate history
- long founder biographies
- every possible service WizeWorks can perform
- generic "Strategy / Innovation / Transformation" service cards
- AI buzzword sections
- technology logo clouds unless relevant to buyer trust
- a contact form above the fold
- multiple competing primary CTA colors

# 26. Measurement Plan

Do not evaluate the page solely on "does it look premium?"

Track meaningful behavior.

Recommended events:

- Hero: Explore our products click
- Hero: View our work click
- Header: Let's build click
- Product-card clicks by product
- Scroll depth
- Case-study clicks
- About/story click
- Insight clicks
- Final CTA click
- Contact-form start
- Contact-form completion

Key homepage metrics:

- product exploration rate
- qualified contact rate
- CTA click-through rate
- case-study engagement
- bounce/engagement rate by traffic source
- mobile conversion behavior
- page performance

A/B tests should focus first on:

1. Hero supporting copy
2. Primary CTA wording
3. Portfolio presentation
4. Proof/results positioning
5. Final CTA language

Do not waste early tests on tiny cosmetic changes.

# 27. Research Basis

The recommended structure is based on current landing-page and conversion guidance from sources including Unbounce, HubSpot, Webflow, CXL, and Nielsen Norman Group.

Key findings used in this recommendation:

- High-converting pages prioritize a clear value proposition and obvious next action.
- Strong visuals should demonstrate the product/experience rather than act as generic decoration.
- A single dominant CTA should have stronger visual hierarchy than secondary actions.
- Social proof is most persuasive when it is credible, specific, relevant, and placed near claims or moments of uncertainty.
- Visitors should not be forced into a high-friction sales interaction before sufficient trust and information exist.
- Forms should minimize unnecessary fields.
- Homepage/corporate pages benefit from useful navigation because many visitors are still researching.
- Professional presentation, accurate information, fast loading, and predictable interaction all contribute to credibility.
- Mobile and performance are conversion considerations, not post-launch refinements.

# 28. Final Direction

WizeWorks should not try to convert by shouting harder.

It should convert by making the visitor think:

> These people clearly know what they're doing.

The page should progressively prove the statement:

> **We make things that work.**

The hero says it.

The portfolio proves WizeWorks makes things.

The product stories prove they solve problems.

The process proves the work is intentional.

The results and customers prove it works.

The company story explains why WizeWorks keeps doing it.

The final CTA then becomes a natural conclusion:

> **Let's build something worth using.**
